Elecciones generales del 29 de noviembre · Demócrata

Vox has collected in 115 proposals in its electoral program for the general elections on next 29 November, with immigration, housing and tax cuts among its main priorities. Under the slogan "It is common sense", the party led by Santiago Abascal proposes to toughen the conditions for the entry and stay of foreigners in Spain, to reserve certain public aid for Spaniards and to eliminate a large part of the current economic and environmental regulation.

The document, of 55 pages, devotes specific sections to the economy, energy, healthcare, education, security, justice and infrastructure. Among its commitments are the repeal of the Housing Law, the establishment of a 0% VAT for the purchase of the first new home, the elimination of arraigo as a route for regularization and the removal of three rules for each new law approved.

It also includes the restoration of the crime of sedition, the repeal of the LOMLOE and the Democratic Memory Law, the maintenance of nuclear power plants and the expansion of non-renewable prison with review. These are the main measures of Vox's program for 29N, which can be consulted in full at the end of this news item.

Immigration and remigration

Immigration policy occupies one of the central blocks of the program. Vox proposes to ban mass regularizations and establish that those who enter Spain irregularly cannot subsequently regularize their situation through that route. It also intends to eliminate rootedness, to toughen the requirements for acquiring Spanish nationality, and to repatriate people who remain in an irregular situation.

The document incorporates a "remigration" plan to facilitate the return to their countries of the foreigners who, according to the criteria included by the party, do not contribute economically or depend on public subsidies.

Likewise, it proposes deporting foreigners who commit serious or repeated crimes and those who, in the words of the program, try to impose customs incompatible with its conception of coexistence. In the case of unaccompanied foreign minors, it defends their return to families or to the guardianship institutions of their countries of origin, taking into account the best interests of the minor.

The training also wants to remove subsidies from organizations it considers to be collaborating with irregular immigration and to suspend development cooperation with countries that do not participate in the control of migratory flows.

National priority in aid, housing and public services

Another axis of the program is the so-called "national priority", with which Vox intends to give Spaniards preference in access to public housing, nursery school spots, dining scholarship grants, transport assistance, and social benefits.

The proposal includes reserving the Minimum Vital Income for Spanish citizens and eliminating social aid and benefits intended for immigrants in an irregular situation.

In healthcare, the party proposes excluding foreigners without legal residence from ordinary public care, except in cases of severe urgency. It also proposes taxing remittances sent abroad.

To implement these measures, Vox announces that it would modify or repeal the rules that make it difficult to implement its criterion of national priority.

Housing: repeal of the law, fewer taxes, and evictions in 24 hours

In terms of housing, Vox bets on increasing construction through a reform of the Land Law, expanding available land, and simplifying urban planning procedures.

Among its commitments is repealing the Housing Law and introducing tax incentives for long-term rentals, as well as deductions for Spanish tenants.

The platform proposes eliminating VAT on the purchase of the first habitual home in new construction and granting a 100% bonus on the tax on property transfers (ITP) for the first habitual home of second-hand property. It would also restore the deduction for investment in the habitual home.

Regarding illegal squatting, it advocates eviction in less than 24 hours, toughening the Criminal Code, and typifying the so-called “squatting” as a fraud crime.

The program adds a ban on renting homes to people in an irregular situation, through prior verification of their legal residence. For non-EU buyers and speculative real-estate operations by funds or companies, it includes more burdensome taxation.

Economy and taxes: lower the IRPF, VAT, and Corporate Tax

Lowering taxes is one of Vox’s main economic proposals. The party announces a reform of the IRPF to simplify it and reduce its burden, with rates that would vary depending on the number of children of the taxpayer or the family.

It also defends lowering the general and reduced VAT rates, with special attention to essential products, and decreasing the corporate tax.

For self-employed workers, it proposes a zero-euro contribution when income does not reach the minimum interprofessional wage. Above that threshold, it establishes a progressive contribution starting from 50 euros, linked to income.

The formation intends to finance these tax cuts by reducing public spending that it calls superfluous, controlling the deficit, and economic growth. However, the program does not quantify the cost of the tax measures nor specify the new IRPF, VAT, or Corporate Tax rates.

Deregulation: eliminate three rules for each new law

Vox proposes a general review of the administrative obligations that affect citizens and businesses. One of its most concrete measures consists in repealing three rules for each new law that is approved, with the obligation to identify the provisions that are eliminated before processing any legislative initiative.

It also wants to put an end to the mandatory prior appointment in public offices, to implement a single digital file, and to extend positive administrative silence as the general rule, with exceptions in matters such as immigration, residence, and nationality.

In the economic sphere, it advocates a single market unit law so that a company or authorized professional in an autonomous community can operate in the rest of Spain without new licenses or registrations.

To this, it adds an audit of the administrations and the gradual reduction of public subsidies to political parties, unions, employers’ associations, and non-governmental organizations.

Energy and industry: keeping nuclear plants and reversing the policies of the Green Pact

In energy policy, Vox proposes repealing the nuclear power plant shutdown calendar and studying the construction of new facilities alongside the existing sites.

It also wants to reduce electricity taxes, remove regulatory limits on investment in electrical grids, and facilitate the exploration and exploitation of mineral and energy resources.

The program expressly includes its rejection of measures derived from the European Green Pact and the Agenda 2030, including restrictions on internal combustion engines and certain taxes on CO₂ emissions.

Training also defends eliminating the requirements of low-emission zones and approving a national reindustrialization plan.

Healthcare and education

In healthcare, Vox proposes implementing a single health card with shared medical records throughout Spain, strengthening primary care, and approving a national plan to reduce waiting lists.

The document includes public-private collaboration when it helps reduce care delays, as well as pay improvements for healthcare professionals and programs to facilitate the return of those who work abroad.

In education, it defends repealing the LOMLOE, establishing a single EVAU, and restoring the revalidations. It also proposes unifying educational curricula, guaranteeing teaching in Spanish, and strengthening free choice of school.

Among the measures are the ban on mobile phones in class, the elimination of halal menus in school cafeterias, the abolition of the Arabic Language and Moroccan Culture program, and the prohibition of the burka and niqab in public spaces.

Furthermore, it wants to set a maximum number of students per classroom who come from other cultures or have insufficient command of Spanish and to create specific leveling classes.

Family, birth rates, and social rights

The program includes a national birth-rate plan that would progressively expand the benefits of large families to all families, starting with the first child, with greater assistance depending on the number of descendants.

It also proposes effective free early childhood education from zero to three years, deductions for school materials and after-school activities, and Social Security contributions financed by the State for Spaniards dedicated to caring for their children.

On matters of rights, Vox advocates protection of life from conception to natural death, conscientious objection by healthcare professionals, and strengthening palliative care.

Likewise, it announces the repeal of gender laws that it considers harmful and their replacement with other rules for the protection of victims of violence.

Justice, security, and the fight against corruption

Among the judicial reforms, the direct election by judges and magistrates of the twelve judicial-origin members of the General Council of the Judiciary stands out.

Vox also proposes restoring the crime of sedition, toughening penalties for misappropriation and banning pardons for certain crimes related to corruption and territorial integrity.

The program includes the outlawing of parties and organizations that, according to the criteria set out in the text, attack the unity and sovereignty of Spain.

On security, it proposes expanding reviewable permanent imprisonment, reducing the age of criminal responsibility for minors, and strengthening the resources of the National Police, the Civil Guard, and the Armed Forces.

It also advocates toughening the criminal protection of national symbols and reforming RTVE.

Rural areas, infrastructure, and transportation

For the primary sector, Vox calls for reviewing the environmental obligations that affect farmers and ranchers, rejecting the trade agreement between the European Union and Mercosur, and introducing mirror clauses in imports.

On infrastructure, it proposes increasing investment in roads, railways, hydraulic networks, and telecommunications.

The program includes an audit of the condition of the rail network, improvements to Cercanías and Media Distancia, and modernization of conventional lines.

Finally, defend a National Water Plan based on the interconnection of river basins, along with new hydraulic works to prevent floods.

What stage is the parliamentary processing of Vox's proposed repeal of the Housing Law at?

There is no record of a Vox bill to fully repeal the Housing Law currently progressing in Congress. Therefore, there is no amendment phase, committee, or Senate stage attributable to that supposed repeal. Law 12/2023, of May 24, on the right to housing, has not been repealed by the identified initiatives.

What this means for its processing

The decisive distinction is between politically proposing a repeal and processing a law that effectively approves it. A bill needs to pass the consideration stage: the vote in which Congress decides whether to start processing its content. After that, amendments, committee work, text approval, and its passage to the Senate may follow.

The sources consulted do not show that a Vox bill specifically aimed at repealing the entire Law 12/2023 has passed that first filter. Nor is there record of its referral to the Senate. Consequently, it would not be correct to describe it as a repeal pending final approval, nor as a text blocked in a specific committee.

Initiatives that may cause confusion

The located documentation records different actions that should be separated to avoid attributing a legal effect they do not have:

The motion approved on September 10, 2026. Congress approved a text from the Popular Group urging the Government to repeal the Housing Law, reform the Land Law, and reduce VAT on the first home. It was a motion, that is, a political request to the Executive, not a repealing law. Therefore, its approval did not eliminate Law 12/2023 nor automatically open processing in the Senate. Also, the text's authorship corresponds to the PP, not Vox (Congress, September 10, 2026).

The bill to modify the Housing Law. File 122/000178, titled “Bill to modify Law 12/2023, of May 24, on the right to housing,” concluded after its consideration was rejected on November 27, 2025. The rejection was published on December 2. However, it should not be presented as a response about a total repeal by Vox: the information consulted does not prove that correspondence between authorship, object, and file.

The housing decrees debated after the dissolution of the Cortes. Vox's request on November 11, 2026, to withdraw their debate from the Permanent Deputation refers to Government decrees, not a bill to repeal the Housing Law. These are different procedures and do not indicate that such repeal is advancing (Demócrata, November 11, 2026).

Practical conclusion

The answer is that no active legislative processing of a total repeal promoted by Vox has been identified. What does appear is a parliamentary repeal request approved at the initiative of the PP and actions on other housing regulations. None of these allow stating that there is a Vox repeal text in the amendment phase or pending in the Senate.

This conclusion does not mean there have been no proposals or repeal claims. It means, more precisely, that the specific legislative procedure referred to in the question is not recorded. For owners and tenants, the consequence is clear: those political requests do not by themselves eliminate the obligations and rights of the Housing Law.

What are the competencies and political trajectory of Santiago Abascal, leader of Vox?

Santiago Abascal Conde, born in Bilbao on April 14, 1976, is president of Vox since September 2014. His trajectory combines local and regional positions, responsibilities in Madrid organizations, and representation in Congress since 2019. To understand his competencies, it is useful to separate three levels: party leadership, parliamentary powers, and responsibilities in European political organizations.

Competencies and responsibilities

As president of Vox, he exercises political representation and directs the party's general strategy. His leadership includes electoral orientation, coordination of leadership, and political dialogue, but does not grant administrative authority over ministries, autonomous communities, or municipalities. In 2024, he was endorsed for another four years with over 90% support, according to Demócrata.

In the parliamentary sphere, he has been a deputy for Madrid in the XIII, XIV, and XV legislatures. During the ordinary functioning of Congress, this status allows participation in debates and votes, promoting initiatives according to the Rules, and controlling the Government through questions and other parliamentary instruments. These powers are subject to procedures and, in certain cases, to group action; they do not equate to an individual capacity to approve laws. His record is available at the Congress of Deputies.

He is also a member of the Permanent Deputation, a body that maintains certain Congress functions when it is dissolved or outside ordinary session periods. In the current electoral context, this responsibility should be distinguished from the Chamber's ordinary activity: he participates in deliberations and votes corresponding to that body, without assuming Government functions.

Since 2024, he presides over Patriots.eu, a European political organization. This responsibility refers to coordination and alliances among parties, not to directing a European Union institution. His election was announced by Vox in November 2024. He also presides over the Disenso Foundation, according to a party communication.

Political trajectory

1994: joining the PP. He joined at 18 and developed his first political stage in the Basque Country.

1999-2007: councilor of Llodio. He represented the PP in the Alava town hall for two terms.

2003-2004: General Assemblies of Álava. He was a procurator in the provincial institution before his regional parliamentary stage.

2004-2009: Basque Parliament. He held a seat for Álava in two periods: January 2004-February 2005 and October 2005-January 2009. These stages are recorded in the RTVE profile.

2006: creation of Denaes. He founded the Foundation for the Defense of the Spanish Nation, which he presided over until 2014.

2010-2013: responsibilities in Madrid. He directed the Data Protection Agency of the Community of Madrid between 2010 and 2012. In 2013, he managed the Foundation for Patronage and Social Sponsorship, according to this biographical review.

2013-2014: leaving the PP and founding Vox. He left the PP at the end of 2013 and participated in founding Vox, registered at the beginning of 2014. He assumed its presidency in September that year. Demócrata reconstructs that stage.

Since 2019: national representation. He entered Congress for Madrid and renewed his seat in the November 2019 and 2023 elections.

In summary, his experience includes municipal, provincial, regional, and national representation, as well as management of public entities and party leadership. His political leadership should not be confused with executive powers: presiding over Vox does not allow him to issue orders to the Government nor exercise ministerial powers.

What legal requirements would be necessary to reserve the Minimum Living Income exclusively for Spanish citizens?

Reserving the Minimum Living Income exclusively for Spanish citizens would require changing its regulation through a law, but that reform would not be enough to make an absolute exclusion valid. It would also have to respect the Constitution, European Union Law, and international treaties. The main obstacle is that there are categories of foreigners protected by equal treatment obligations that Spain cannot unilaterally eliminate.

1. Modify the law regulating the MLI

It would be necessary to amend Law 19/2021, of December 20, establishing the Minimum Living Income, to introduce Spanish nationality as an access requirement. An administrative instruction, a Social Security resolution, or a regulation contrary to the legally defined beneficiaries would not suffice.

The ordinary route would be an ordinary law approved by the General Courts, involving both Congress and Senate. An organic law is not required merely for regulating a social benefit or affecting foreigners. Any other legal provision recognizing equal access and incompatible with the reform would also have to be adapted.

A royal decree-law would require justifying an extraordinary and urgent need and respecting the limits of Article 86 of the Constitution. A structural exclusion of foreigners would pose additional difficulties due to its impact on rights in Title I; it would not be a shortcut to bypass those limits.

2. Pass constitutional review

The Constitution does not require automatic equal treatment of Spaniards and foreigners in all benefits, but neither does it allow any difference. Article 13.1 refers to treaties and law; that referral does not grant unlimited freedom to the legislator.

STC 236/2007 explains that foreigners do not necessarily have the same rights as Spaniards. The scope of differences depends on the affected right and its constitutional guarantees.

The reform would have to justify a legitimate purpose and a reasonable and proportionate difference. The analysis would combine Articles 13 and 14 with personal dignity in Article 10.1, the prohibition of arbitrariness in Article 9.3, and social protection in Article 41. Its unconstitutionality cannot be presumed in advance, but excluding by nationality residents in identical need situations would require especially strong justification.

3. Respect European and international guarantees

This is the decisive limit for a truly exclusive reservation:

Union citizens: the non-discrimination principle and Directive 2004/38 protect equal treatment in situations covered by European law. There are exceptions for certain periods or residence conditions, but no general authorization to exclude all non-Spaniards.

Long-term residents: Directive 2003/109 recognizes equal treatment in Social Security and social assistance, with specific limits. Those exceptions do not allow presuming a complete exclusion of the MLI as valid.

Persons with international protection: European guarantees and Geneva Convention obligations must be respected. Asylum seekers also have reception guarantees, although these do not automatically equate to a right to the MLI.

Compatibility with the revised European Social Charter and other applicable treaties would also have to be checked. A Spanish constitutional reform alone would not eliminate European or international obligations.

4. Practical consequence

An absolute exclusion of all foreigners would not be legally resolved by approving a national law. To respect the higher framework, mandatory exceptions would have to be maintained; therefore, it would cease to be an exclusively Spanish reservation. Otherwise, the measure would be exposed to constitutional challenge, European procedures, and, when applicable, judicial non-application due to conflict with Union Law.
